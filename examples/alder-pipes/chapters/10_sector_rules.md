# Alder Pipes · Sector rules

## Summary
A water board awards each lot to the lowest price that comes with a bank guarantee of a tenth of the lot. The builder pays wages, pipe and machines from the first month, and the board pays after it accepts a finished section. A section takes four to six months, so every builder carries months of its own spending on each lot. Past performance works as a gate: once a builder passes it, only price and guarantee count. Receivables of a quarter to a third of revenue are normal at year end in this trade.

## Evidence
- A board awards a lot to the lowest price that comes with a guarantee of 10% of the lot. [Westland general conditions, January 2024, p. 4]
- A board pays within 60 days of accepting a section, and a section takes four to six months to build. [Westland general conditions, January 2024, p. 7]
- Past performance is a pass or fail gate, and adds nothing to a bid that passes it. [Westland general conditions, January 2024, p. 4]
- A consortium splits the guarantee by each partner's share. [Westland general conditions, January 2024, p. 5]
- A firm may put in only one bid on a lot, alone or in a consortium. [Westland general conditions, January 2024, p. 5]
- Most sections are accepted in autumn, so receivables peak at the year end. [national builders' association yearbook 2025, June 2025]

### Figures that read differently here
- Receivables of 31% of revenue: an outsider reads slow payers; in this trade a quarter to a third of revenue at year end is normal, because the boards accept most sections in autumn. [national builders' association yearbook 2025, June 2025]
- Debts that grow with revenue: an outsider reads borrowing; here they are mostly bills from pipe suppliers on open sections, matched by receivables. [national builders' association yearbook 2025, June 2025]
- Twenty years of acceptance records: an outsider reads an edge in every tender; here the record only passes the gate. [Westland general conditions, January 2024, p. 4]
